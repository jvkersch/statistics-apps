Wilcoxon rank-sum test

Rank all observations together and add up the ranks of group A: that sum is the statistic W. If the groups do not differ, the group labels are just a random shuffle. Shuffle them yourself to build up the distribution of W under the null hypothesis.

Try this

  1. For each data set, is the observed W unusually small?
  2. The proportion of shuffles with W at most the observed value estimates a one-sided p-value. How stable is it after 100 shuffles?
