Sixty-two mice

Sixty-two cards, one per mouse. Thirteen are dealt to the E. coli group, and then twenty-seven mice develop a tumour, chosen completely at random with no regard for who was exposed. Exposure is fixed by the experiment; only the tumours are shuffled. That is the null hypothesis, made concrete.

Read your T out when your lecturer asks for it.

Try this

  1. The real experiment found T = 8. Shuffle a few times: how often does chance alone hand you 8 or more?
  2. Once the whole class has pooled its draws, that proportion is an estimate of P(T ≥ 8), the answer to "could this result be due to chance?"
